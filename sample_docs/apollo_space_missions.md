# Apollo Lunar Exploration Program

## Overview of the Program
The Apollo program was an American human spaceflight initiative carried out by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) between 1961 and 1972. Its definitive accomplishment was landing the first humans on the Moon during the Apollo 11 mission on July 20, 1969.

## Launch Vehicle: The Saturn V
The primary launch vehicle utilized for crewed lunar voyages was the Saturn V rocket, engineered under the leadership of Wernher von Braun at the Marshall Space Flight Center. The Saturn V was a three-stage, liquid-fueled super heavy-lift rocket standing 110.6 meters (363 feet) tall and generating approximately 34.5 meganewtons (7.89 million pounds-force) of sea-level thrust upon ignition.

- **Stage 1 (S-IC)**: Powered by five Rocketdyne F-1 engines burning RP-1 kerosene and liquid oxygen (LOX).
- **Stage 2 (S-II)**: Powered by five Rocketdyne J-2 engines burning liquid hydrogen (LH2) and LOX.
- **Stage 3 (S-IVB)**: Powered by a single J-2 engine, responsible for orbital insertion and Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI).

## Major Missions and Crew Details
### Apollo 11
Launched on July 16, 1969, carrying Commander Neil Armstrong, Command Module Pilot Michael Collins, and Lunar Module Pilot Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin. Armstrong and Aldrin landed the Lunar Module, designated *Eagle*, in the Sea of Tranquility (*Mare Tranquillitatis*) on July 20, 1969. Armstrong stepped onto the lunar surface at 02:56 UTC on July 21, 1969. The crew collected 21.55 kilograms (47.5 pounds) of lunar surface material.

### Apollo 13
Launched on April 11, 1970, with crew members Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert, and Fred Haise. At approximately 56 hours into the flight, an oxygen tank in the Service Module ruptured due to damaged electrical insulation. The mission aborted the planned Fra Mauro landing. By utilizing the Lunar Module *Aquarius* as a makeshift life raft and executing a circumlunar trajectory around the Moon, the crew returned safely to Earth, splashing down in the South Pacific Ocean on April 17, 1970.

### Apollo 17
Launched on December 7, 1972, Apollo 17 was the final mission of the Apollo lunar landing program. Crewed by Eugene Cernan, Ronald Evans, and Harrison Schmitt (the first trained geologist sent to the Moon), the mission explored the Taurus-Littrow valley. Cernan was the last human to walk on the lunar surface during the 20th century. The mission returned 110.5 kilograms of lunar rocks.
