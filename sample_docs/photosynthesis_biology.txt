BIOLOGICAL PRINCIPLES OF PHOTOSYNTHESIS

1. Introduction and Chemical Equation
Photosynthesis is the biochemical process by which oxygenic photoautotrophs convert radiant solar energy into chemical energy stored within covalent bonds of carbohydrates. The net balanced stoichiometric equation for oxygenic photosynthesis is:
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + photons -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2

The process takes place inside specialized plant organelles termed chloroplasts, which are derived evolutionary descendants of endosymbiotic cyanobacteria.

2. Chloroplast Structure
Each chloroplast is enveloped by a double-membrane system containing:
- The Stroma: A protein-rich fluid matrix containing soluble enzymes, circular chloroplast DNA, and ribosomes.
- Thylakoids: Flattened membrane-bound sac structures stacked into disk columns called grana. The internal space bounded by thylakoid membranes is known as the thylakoid lumen.

3. Light-Dependent Reactions
The light-dependent reactions occur within the thylakoid membranes. Solar photons are absorbed by pigment-protein complexes known as Photosystem II (PSII / P680) and Photosystem I (PSI / P700).
- Photolysis of Water: At the oxygen-evolving complex of PSII, water molecules are split into electrons, protons (H+), and diatomic oxygen (O2):
  2 H2O -> 4 H+ + 4 e- + O2
- Electron Transport Chain (ETC): Excited electrons traverse plastoquinone, the cytochrome b6f complex, and plastocyanin, pumping protons from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen. This electrochemical proton gradient drives ATP synthase to synthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate via photophosphorylation.
- Reduction of NADP+: Electrons arriving at PSI are re-energized by photons and transferred through ferredoxin to NADP+ reductase, reducing NADP+ into NADPH.

4. The Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin-Benson Cycle)
The Calvin Cycle occurs within the stroma and utilizes the ATP and NADPH generated by light reactions to fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into triose phosphate sugars.
It proceeds in three phases:
- Phase 1: Carbon Fixation. The enzyme Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase (RuBisCO) catalyzes the carboxylation of ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), yielding unstable 6-carbon intermediates that immediately split into 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA).
- Phase 2: Reduction. 3-PGA is phosphorylated by ATP and reduced by NADPH to form glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P).
- Phase 3: Regeneration of RuBP. Complex enzymatic rearrangements consuming additional ATP convert five molecules of G3P back into three molecules of RuBP, enabling continuous rounds of carbon assimilation. For every three CO2 molecules fixed, one net G3P molecule exits the cycle toward glucose synthesis.
